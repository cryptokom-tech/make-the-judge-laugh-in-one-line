# Candidates — Make the Judge Laugh in One Line

Brief: one funny line, 200 characters or fewer. Judge weighs, in order:
a joke a stranger gets in one read, surprise, tightness, freshness, and
clean read-aloud. Each candidate addresses the judge directly and carries
one specific emotion plus a turn.

## Candidates

- A. Judge, I don't fear rejection—I've framed so much of it I'm running out of wall.
- B. Judge, my confidence arrived early, saw you, and called in sick.
- C. Judge, I brought confidence, but security confiscated it as an obvious fake.
- D. Judge, I'm not nervous; I'm pre-disappointed, which saves us both the suspense.
- E. Judge, I rehearsed being spontaneous until it sounded memorized.
- F. Judge, I tried to act natural, but my personality asked for a stunt double.
- G. Judge, I've heard silence so often I started charging it rent.
- H. Judge, my therapist says I crave approval, so congratulations—you're now part of my treatment plan.

## Pairwise notes (local judging)

- A vs B: B is more judge-specific, but A has the fresher image; the wall
  punch lands last and cannot be predicted from the setup. A wins.
- A vs C: C's confiscation turn is clear, yet "obvious fake" explains the
  joke; A's framed-rejection image shows instead of explaining. A wins.
- A vs D: D is witty, but "pre-disappointed" is a coinage a stranger must
  unpack; A reads clean in one pass. A wins.
- A vs E: E's paradox is tight and fresh, but it is a clever observation
  more than a laugh; A gives a concrete visual payoff. A wins.
- A vs F: F is fresh, though the stunt-double turn arrives early and the
  line coasts after it; A's laugh is the final phrase. A wins.
- A vs G: G is original but the emotion stays implied; A names rejection
  outright, then turns it. A wins.
- A vs H: H names approval-seeking well, but the therapist setup is heavy
  for one line; A is tighter with the same self-deprecating emotion. A wins.
- B vs C (for second place): B is tighter and aimed at the judge in the
  room; C needs the security image to do all the work. B wins.
- E vs F: E is tighter; F is fresher. E wins on tightness, the earlier
  criterion after surprise.

## Winner

A — "Judge, I don't fear rejection—I've framed so much of it I'm running out of wall."

It names one specific emotion (fear of rejection, denied), addresses the
judge directly, sets up a boast, then breaks it with a concrete image whose
laugh lands on the last words. 80 characters, one clean read aloud.
